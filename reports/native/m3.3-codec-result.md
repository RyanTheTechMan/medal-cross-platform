The helper now publishes `gpuDevices`, `gpuCodecs` and `encoderOptions` through the existing `setKV` path instead of adding a parallel UI. `m3.3-codec-protocol-selftest.json` records the actual signed Electron → WebSocket → helper result: GPU `Apple M5 Max`, codecs `H264` and `H265`, and encoder option `GPU`.

## Hardware-required encoder probe

Command:

`DEVELOPER_DIR=/Applications/Xcode-beta.app/Contents/Developer ./build-macos/native/backends/macos/native_port_macos_encoder_probe`

Result: exit 0. `m3.3-videotoolbox-encoder-probe.json` is the full output. Hardware-required H.264 and HEVC sessions both confirmed `UsingHardwareAcceleratedVideoEncoder=true`; four submitted real-time frames for each codec produced four callbacks within 100 ms without an explicit flush, no dropped frame or callback error, and valid `avcC`/`hvcC` codec configuration. Real-time mode, disabled frame reordering and quality-priority mode were accepted.

This host registered Apple hardware and software H.264/HEVC encoders but no AV1 encoder. A hardware-required AV1 session failed at creation with OSStatus `-12908`. The same machine's AV1 decode capability is not evidence of AV1 encode capability. Consequently AV1 is omitted from the imported client's available codec list on this host and an explicit AV1 capture request fails rather than silently falling back.

## Real signed-client HEVC capture

Manual prerequisite actually performed: the interactive user chose one display in the macOS ScreenCaptureKit system picker. The stable signing identities were unchanged and TCC was not reset or edited. No microphone, camera, input-monitoring or Medal login was requested.

The final test launched the signed app with an isolated `artifacts/test-profiles/m3.3-hevc-final` profile, `NATIVE_PORT_CAPTURE_SELFTEST_KIND=display`, `NATIVE_PORT_CAPTURE_SELFTEST_CODEC=H265` and `NATIVE_PORT_CAPTURE_SELFTEST_BITRATE_MBPS=10`. `m3.3-hevc-display-passed.json` is the result.

- ScreenCaptureKit enumerated content and the selected source independently reported `display`.
- Effective video was 1920×802 at requested 60 fps and 10,000,000 bits/s.
- 441 image frames entered VideoToolbox and 441 HEVC frames completed over 8.030274125 seconds; encode failures were zero.
- VideoToolbox confirmed the hardware encoder.
- The replay snapshot retained 441 HEVC packets, 4,303,344 bytes, five keyframes and five packets carrying the active `hvcC` configuration generation.
- One idle frame and subsequent `source_resumed` transition were observed; the idle marker was not encoded as a duplicate image.
- Requested stop completed and the recorder exited normally through the imported client's shutdown route.

Two retained failed iterations explain the selected encoder policy. `m3.3-hevc-display-2.json` proves Apple's advertised High Quality preset produced a valid hardware HEVC keyframe/configuration but held subsequent static-source frames in its look-ahead window. `m3.3-hevc-display-3.json` proves attempts to force that preset to zero delay were rejected with OSStatus `-12900`; the later property-specific probe identified `MaxFrameDelayCount`. The final configuration therefore uses real-time mode, no frame reordering and `PrioritizeEncodingSpeedOverQuality=false` without the High Quality preset. This preserves quality priority while avoiding the observed rolling-capture stall.
