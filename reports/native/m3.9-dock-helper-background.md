# M3.9 Dock helper identity fix

Date: 2026-09-19 (UTC)

## Diagnosis

The extra Dock item was not the native recorder. It was an Electron utility
process launched from `Contents/Frameworks/Electron Helper.app` and registered
by Launch Services as a foreground application with the display name
`Electron Helper`. The native recorder was already a `UIElement` and did not
create a Dock item.

The nested Electron helper plists contained `LSUIElement`, but the development
app builder did not enforce that field and Launch Services had a stale helper
registration from an earlier bundle activation. The running process inventory
showed the helper as a second foreground `Medal` app entry, while the recorder
was correctly listed as `Native Medal Recorder` / `UIElement`.

## Correction

`tools/build_macos_development_app.py` now explicitly writes
`LSUIElement = true` for every Electron utility, renderer, GPU and plugin
helper during every deterministic build. This preserves the host's single
visible Dock identity without changing helper process roles or bypassing
macOS security. The exact nested helper bundles were re-registered with
Launch Services after installation, and the app was cleanly restarted.

## Live verification

The rebuilt app was installed at `/Applications/Medal.app` and strict/deep
code-signature verification passed. After clean launch, `lsappinfo list`
contained only these Medal-owned application entries:

```text
"Medal"                  bundleID=com.squirrel.medal.medal              type=Foreground
"Native Medal Recorder"  bundleID=com.squirrel.medal.medal.recorder     type=UIElement
```

No `Electron Helper` foreground entry remained. The process list still showed
the normal Electron helper utility/GPU/renderer children, but they no longer
had an independent Dock identity.

The exact build output is retained in
`reports/native/m3.9-dock-helper-background-build.log`.

## Voice-trigger test clarification

The imported `Start Test` control is Medal's **Voice Trigger Test**, not a
microphone sidetone/monitor. Its recovered UI text says “Start a test session
and say one of your trigger phrases to check Medal can hear you” and the
listening state says “Listening on microphone …”. It intentionally detects a
phrase and does not play the microphone back through the speakers. Visible
microphone activity therefore confirms the test input path; lack of audible
echo is expected. A fresh normal clip with microphone enabled remains the
separate test for encoded microphone audio and playback.
